Why Has Bodhi-Dharma Left for the East

Posted: 2025-02-24

Why Has Bodhi-Dharma Left for the East? is a film directed, written, and produced by Bang Yong-kyun. It is certainly a lesser known movie in 2025, having only around 1,500 ratings on Letterboxd. But, it is also known for being the one of the first South Korean movies to receive a theatrical release in the United States. This movie was created at a time before Korea had a substantial film canon, and in many facets is a movie antithetical to monetary success: it is uncompromising, slow, heavily spiritual, and reflective. This movie is in cohort with directors like Tarkovsky in its philosophical and spiritual spearheads, but in many ways this movie is just as inaccessible (and in some manners even more so.) This movie lacks a clear narrative, and can be thought to be more like a Koan (a paradoxical riddle intrinsic to the Zen tradition that challenges our logical thinking and can induce spontaneous enlightenment or clarity) than a movie. But, if one is able to let go of their intuitions about how a movie ought to be and dedicate the intention espoused in the film, what you receive is perhaps one of the most important experiences you will have in your life.

The Last Showgirl

Posted: 2025-02-24

The Last Showgirl is an American film released in 2024 starring Pamela Anderson. Pamela plays Shelly Gardner, a long term Showgirl at La Razzle Dazzle. She possesses an innate struggle with her occupation and her role as a mother, and also the general stigmatization of showgirl business. Such struggle manifests in prejudice, self-hatred, and a desire for amending her relationship with her daughter and other dancers. Dr. Strangelove

Posted: 2025-02-24

I watched this movie under the supervision of my movie sensei Knox.

Dr. Strangelove is a military man’s wet dream, but it is certainly not a blonde haired bombshell. No, Dr. Strangelove makes the argument that the military men’s hyper masculinity and love for military superiority is so perverse that it may as well be a form of…. Strange love. Phallic imagery is on display throughout this movie, such as the airplace refueling scene, and the B52 dangling superimposed in the sky. It is very reminiscent of Thomas Pynchon, in which he equates the American rocket to the phallus. Even the president is talking like he is in bed with the Russian president.

Annihilation

Posted: 2025-02-24

I watched this movie under the supervision of my movie senseis Eben and Knox.

Annihilation. This movie has a somewhat unlikable cast of characters, and it feels as though there are a lot of gaps in this movie’s motivation. Yes, I get it that these characters are incredibly troubled and thus are willing to essentially go on a suicide mission to obtain more information about the light house, but why only these 5 people? I know they are all crazy and traumatized, but still. Maybe I should care less about the lens cap being on the camera, but I feel like this movie leaves a lot of questions regarding the characters and their motives. Another gripe I have is that the dialogue is incredibly simplistic. Though, it could be argued that this simple dialogue is rather sparse intentionally, which could be lending to the general puzzle that is this film. The characters at times felt somewhat flat, which led me to feeling slightly disconnected from the film at times.
