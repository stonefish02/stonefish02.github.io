Inner & Outer

Over the past few years, I have been keeping a dream journal. I think I began this journal because I had some faith in the fact that doing so would be good, whether it being a basis for further self-understanding in itself, or simply an indirect way of developing self-awareness, and I have been keeping it ever since. Over those years, I have done a little bit of rereading, and one of the emergent patterns of these dreams is a softening of the boundary between inner and outer spaces, not only in a physical sense, but also in a more general, and abstract sense. In the specific way, I find that I never have a very clear distinction of when a room ends, or where it even begins. The retro futurism of the cafe where I was arguing with my mother (or at least, some form of her) forms a soft, and in most cases, altogether unnoticeable gradient where I am now slamming a car door so hard the windows break, the sun refracting in the fractals of my wrath and peltering me with the regret of past summer days, those moments in Lubbock where I felt no rest riding my bicycle back and forth from Texas Tech campus, doused in sweat, subsumed in the blueness of the sky, its color radiating out from that moment into a fuzzy neighborhood, a cloudy portion in the (foolishly) imposed interval of time in my mind.

I often have dreams where I am someone else. It is not simply me being merely trapped in another person’s body (though, that has happened too), it is something more substantial, where the character of my entire experience seems to shift: not only am I convinced I am that person whose body I am now possessing, but the comparison between who I once was and who I am now does not even exist, so far a distance between who I am and the dream-self was. There have been dreams where I am Tony Soprano (which, rather paradoxically, what my subconscious interprets Tony Soprano to be, and what his own experience must be – in the same breath confining me to my experience), there have been dreams where I have been a woman with a family, but also times where the boundaries are not so loose: that I am supremely capable version of myself (or, perhaps, cousin Sam running the gambit on the movie aristocracy), that I am a physically inept child who allowed their car stolen by long lost friends, whose strikes and admonitions are only toasts and discourse to the ceremony of sadism, my tears streaming through the open, sometimes air-conditioned air, confetti and jewels decorating and garnishing the decor like an art-noveau mirror inverting a constantly swinging grandfather clock, whose tune I can never remember, whose lyrics can only be understood in the moment they most acutely existed. This is what I would find as I became conscious in that old Immanuel Lutheran playground, where all of a sudden I stepped into the room and I began to split myself apart into my atomic constituents, passing through the heavy sieve that would funnel me into that mirror world. I find that the only semblance of true understanding I find with myself are in those lucid situations, and especially, those moments when I become conscious of my being in a dream, and the subsequent desires and wishes that are underlying the first few words or thoughts I have within that heavenly sphere. I am not quite sure if this is a feature of my brain, or simply what it has become attuned to: as of recently, what seems to be my core problem of existence is my own originality as a human being. What is it exactly that distinguishes my thoughts from others, what ideas could be distinctly described as my own, if not being the constituents of other experiences or ideas from people?

As I have continued my journey in reading Proust, I continue believing that I have never had an original thought before. If it is true that our social selves are a combination of other people’s opinions and thoughts about ourselves (our own included), then how could anyone truly distinguish themselves from the sinews and blood of these threads of idealism? Though I would say I have some semblance of that essence of my own being, there are other moments where it feels as though the cork is removed, and I find myself to be in some sort of a dream in my waking life. Especially in the first few months I have been living in Japan, the uprooting of my life uprooted my habits. This unearthed some of the most uncomfortable experiences I can remember in recency, almost as if, unaware of the parasite that was nestled inside of our physical body, one day we become aware of a certain pain or twinge that we correlated with an unknown movement, we in one fell swoop resolve to remove the damned creature immediately, it burrowing itself even further within us, plunging its claws and holding on with its utmost ferocity. Though it may seem to be our salve which cures our pain, its removal may come to draw us even further into former habits. Having moved to Japan, I find that I have tried to reinstitute the habits of times past where I felt content and happy, however, that lovely, ghostly orchid has not died with the shattering of the vase, but in the same breath of gaining this humble abode, lost it, but desires it no longer, and needs a new containment otherwise it will wilt and fade away. I am being forced to change in my intellectual, or spiritual pursuits.

Our minds being perhaps vessels, or certainly, large families and collections of those vases one may find in a flower shop, of varying capacity and durability, time being the violent youth drunk on their own indifferences running around kicking and smashing things: it is no wonder the memories of the ridiculous and off-puttingly specific are the survivors even amongst that Chinese porcelain we have spent so much time protecting, our conscious declaration “I will remember this for the rest of my life” being but petty grovelling before the sublime adolescent. When I try to strike upon an original idea, I may stop reading for a little bit, may leave my phone in the other room, trying to close out all possible distractions to rail and charter my will and mind upon the same tracks. But, this thought that I had just now, is it not simply based on a former impression?

I keep running back to the Platonic idea that there is no such thing as actual learning, that in some capacity, everything is a reflection back to some original set of ideas that we as human beings obtained in our early impressions. But, do those always remain within us, those immutable crystallizations upon which everything is built, or is the space an affine one, in which the base point is donned in deterministic form: it reads to me as if you are in some sort of room, or maybe, you are in one of those vases from the flower shop: you are fixed to some sort of contraption or chair which seems to be powered from your own mind, and in each instance, a situation occurs to you, maybe even a thought appears in your head and in that instant, the chair orients you to a certain shade in that sommerso vase which colors your vision as you peer outside to see that visual movie of the situation play. However, you realize you are not in any kind of vase, that the sommerso glass could never fall like scales from your eyes, but is the very essence by which you were able to see in the first place? In some way, then, to perceive is to be constrained in the first place, to be able to see would be to place yourself inside of the sommerso vase, composed of the dead entrails of people and places long dead and past, that luxurious blue and purple fading into each other, it being merely its crystallization, of an amber of thought and experience.

It’s no surprise that Japan seems to have exacerbated some of these dream-like preoccupations. The distinction between outer vs. inner ranges from strikingly stark, to jaggedly non-existent. In many malls in Japan, I find that there is a far larger emphasis on verticality as compared to American malls, as when you exit the escalator, you will enter into a shop within a few steps, you will be categorically classifying each of the shoes, pumas with two toes, mustard shoes, on-clouds, converse with 3 inch platforms, shoelaces, the attendant asks you politely if you need anything, you say it is okay, and have barely realized that you have stepped into a store. There are no doors, and there are certainly no portals: entrances are in corners, as if some heavenly figure had dropped their glass cube and simply allowed it to roll over onto its open side, spilling out its commodities and contents into the walkway. You are in Shinjuku, and you see a sign which reads as Yobodashi Camera in bright red font, and you walk up the escalator. It is easy to walk around, and with each movement you are effortlessly catapulted into the next tangential thought. You may see the speakers outside, which are adjacent to the movie and CD sections, which have effortlessly blended into the IEM sections, where there are cables and mobile record players, there are headphones and there are MP3 players. You might stick around to look at it, and see the light of projectors in the dimness of a room, and pens roaming the wall where you see a PILOT logo which entices your view. In this dream-like phase, you are simply susceptible to the processes of your mind, its convenience and its sheer availability leads one to a sort of hedonistic heaven in that moment – as rarely ever is money a concern. The images and language are rolling in your mind, as you roll along on the road to the next object: the light is drowning you, the endless rows of LED lights make the warm wood of a table enticing, as if in the languor of your vision, one finally finds a self-assured pleasure in where your gaze lies, you lie in the couch which relaxes your gaze. You sip your vending machine water, and finally feel some respite, but still, you must go back out into the world. As you click X on your application, you realize that the boundary between movement and intellect were removed in that moment. There is no dualistic metaphysic diffused throughout this universe, as it turns out that traversal in Shinjuku transcends into a largely linguistic process, where words are enough to guide one to what they believe they desire: it is convenient and seamless, enticing and endless, but if one were to all of a sudden become aware of the anchor of their identity, perhaps of their virtues and their resistance to consumerism embroiled in them by self-knowledge, that glorious virtue which impelled the beginning of their journey into the world, one also becomes aware of the simultaneous pull which is exerted by wanting and desiring, one becomes aware of the lack of control they actually had in this process, that in that brief moment of awareness, they were being guided and coddled into a dream amassed by those caught in similar delusions of grandeur. But this desiring is no external force pulling you towards something, it is simply another part of your essence which pulls you taut, tensed with this by the anchor of our personality, or perhaps even our soul: you seem to grow skeptical of the prolific power of the soul over the body in those moments, whether even such a thing could exist.

As you made your journey back home, away from the store, you passed through undulating train stations of nigh-infinite amounts of people. Not in so far as it is too dense to count, but in so far as it is impossible to see their end, perhaps a more countable infinity, but one as incomprehensible as the last. Scanning in at the gate is as smooth as leaving and entering, you were outside previously and now you are leading up the escalator. As you are hovering into the next chunk of the world, the humid, dull air of Tokyo wafts onto the platform, the pittering of the rain taps like those warm dinks and dents that lull you back into bed such as on those sleepy dreary days, distorted by the vibration of a sort of platform, the one you lie on now where those endless slews of figures peer into their phones, that technological, crystalline jewel with a small beating brain, transparent with your desires and hopes, you are displaying it to the world, yet at the same time, shy away. Maybe, after some time, as you approach the suburbs, as you try to recount what just unfolded before you, you may take a breath and regain some amount of your individuality. You feel yourself in your body, you may feel as though you are again inside of your head.

When I finally felt myself to be “myself” again, I became aware of a certain helplessness as I wandered around Tokyo. As mentioned in my first blog post, there is no time to stop walking in Tokyo. There are no places to sit down (the exception being the Nitori furniture shop at 8 o’clock on a weekend), though, you may be able to find a place to eat (anything you like, it is endless after all,) and take off your load there. It is a series of constant movements, and finding stillness can be extraordinarily difficult, even parks and shrines are crowded and overloaded. You are reminded of the constant flux of mind, but also simultaneously deafened to it as well, as you are no longer a human being in Tokyo. This place made me more sensitive to my pre-preceived sense of determinism, and afforded me a realistic framing of Gaia theory, in which human beings are constituents of a system constantly in homeostasis. Is originality possible? Yes, but maybe it will not make me any more free. In Tokyo, there existed a phase space of all possible paths and orientations: it is a place not where dreams come true, but where the distance between dreams and reality, between intellect and material are asymptotically approaching each other. Maybe the limit will blow up in finite time, and in Tokyo, one is simply no more than their mind, an idealist heaven.
