Inner & Outer

Posted: 2026-10-01

Over the past few years, I have been keeping a dream journal. I think I began this journal because I had some faith in the fact that doing so would be good, whether it being a basis for further self-understanding in itself, or simply an indirect way of developing self-awareness, and I have been keeping it ever since. Over those years, I have done a little bit of rereading, and one of the emergent patterns of these dreams is a softening of the boundary between inner and outer spaces, not only in a physical sense, but also in a more general, and abstract sense. In the specific way, I find that I never have a very clear distinction of when a room ends, or where it even begins. The retro futurism of the cafe where I was arguing with my mother (or at least, some form of her) forms a soft, and in most cases, altogether unnoticeable gradient where I am now slamming a car door so hard the windows break, the sun refracting in the fractals of my wrath and peltering me with the regret of past summer days, those moments in Lubbock where I felt no rest riding my bicycle back and forth from Texas Tech campus, doused in sweat, subsumed in the blueness of the sky, its color radiating out from that moment into a fuzzy neighborhood, a cloudy portion in the (foolishly) imposed interval of time in my mind.

Gaman (我慢）

Posted: 2026-10-01

Trial by fire. There is hardship. A lot of it. The most special contributor to this hardship is the loneliness that I feel here in Japan. One part is cultural, another section is life in the big city. I do not want to lend too much information regarding my location, because the people who ought to know where I am already know, and anyone who doesn’t is probably not in an immediacy that would mentally condone me sharing that information. But, I live in one of the large metropolitan areas in Japan, a swath of land which is far larger than any place I have been to in my entire life, and one which encodes its own adjustment. There are certain features of big city life which are nice and quite convenient. Postal services deliver within 1 or 2 days, there are multiple conbini on each block, there is no shortage of things to do in the realm of entertainment; yet there is an omnipresent and looming rigidity of this society which feels impossible to break through. I explored this a little bit in my comments on the Japanese waste disposal system, but another one of its symptoms is that it feels like it is incredibly difficult to connect with people here, even beyond just a language barrier.

My Experience with the Japanese Waste Management System

Posted: 2026-08-08

This is going to be less of a blog and more of a filler for the next post. I do not have enough ideas for the next blog post, but I have enough energy to write more. So, I figured I would take a slight turn.

Up until my High School education, I rarely, if ever, thought about my consumption and waste disposal patterns — it, like many features of adolescent life, has its responsibility transferred directly and entirely to one’s parents, absolving many young adults at a point in life where they probably have enough consciousness to be accountable to take their own trash out. I believe the first time I ever started thinking about my consumption patterns were when I entered college, when I had the first semblance of independence and the largest extent of freedom I have ever had; independent freedom, especially in one’s young adult life, gives one the ability to fall in similar measure with flourish. Recycling and responsible consumption habits, then, are sort of special: one does not tangibly benefit from recycling in a material way, but indirectly, from the intentional sacrificing of one’s freedom for some perceived “greater good”. Given the scale on which waste accumulates, one might not even see purported benefits in their lifetime, notwithstanding the fact that recycling is not something where one gains, but rather, a force which staves: it is a preservation of what is now, from decay.

Moby Dick is a Pink Gilded Breakfast Ticket (Blog 1)

Posted: 0001-01-01

So this will be the first blog entry. It feels a little bit rushed, but for the time being, it’s really just about getting the ball rolling, and getting into the habit of making semi-frequent posts. That being said, I am not so certain if I will make it a weekly endeavor. Maybe it will become an event that I post when I feel like a sufficient amount happens, interspersed with the occasional essay or thought to keep things fresh. This blog is here to give people my experience as a teacher in Hyogo-ken, but more broadly as an American living in Japan. Whether this move was an escape, or me running along the path I need to traverse is not fully clear to me at this point; I think that line is rather thin. This blog is apart of a broader mindfulness program on my part, and as such, some of the posts might be a little dense, perhaps even terse at times. But, for those who are not my direct friends and family, I can also guarantee you that it will be worth reading outside of seeing what is happening to me. I am hoping to develop more of a knack for writing non-fiction. Where this will lead me in my future career endeavors is yet to be fully realized, but many good things turn out to be materially fruitless.
